# How business changed in a decade (notes for time.html)

Source: Forbes Global 2000 (2025) 10-year history, approximate fiscal years 2015–2024, nominal US$; World Bank (GDP, market cap, listed companies).

## Quotable facts
1. Constant cohort: **1,278** of today's Global 2000 firms with full 2015–24 data (10 firms with unit errors dropped).
2. Cohort revenue **$29.2T → $45.0T** (×1.54, 4.9%/yr).
3. Cohort profit **$2.09T → $4.11T** (×1.97, 7.8%/yr). Profit outran revenue.
4. Net margin **7.1% → 9.1%**, with a low of 6.3% in 2020 and a peak of ~10% in 2021.
5. World GDP rose ×1.47 (current US$) over the same years, so big firms grew a bit faster than the economy and their profits much faster.
6. **14.7%** of cohort firms had lower revenue in 2024 than 2015. Median growth was +51%.
7. Fastest growers (≥$5B in 2015): **Nvidia ×26** ($5.0B → $130.5B), Meta ×9.2, Alibaba ×9.1, BYD ×9, Broadcom ×7.8.
8. Revenue CAGR by country: **Saudi Arabia 11.0%, Canada 8.2%, India 7.6%** (39 firms, $542B → $1.05T; profit 10.5%/yr), US 6.5%, China 6.3%, Japan 1.9% (weak yen).
9. Rank mobility: Amazon 43rd → 2nd by revenue, Alphabet 75th → 9th, Microsoft 63rd → 19th, Cigna 202nd → 20th. Sinopec 2nd → 5th, Shell 4th → 17th.
10. Profit pool, sector share (2015–16 avg → 2023–24 avg): **Energy & Utilities 3.5% → 10.6%** (oil cycle), **Technology 12.3% → 17.4%**, Financials 37.4% → 34.4%, Telecom & Media 6.1% → 2.9%, Healthcare 7.7% → 5.2%.
11. **Tech takeover:** tech's share of cohort profit went 12.4% (2015) → 18.5% (2024) on roughly 8–9% of revenue. Tech is **26.8%** of Global 2000 market value in 2025 (no market-value history in the source).
12. Revenue per employee (932 firms with headcount 2017–24): **$455K → $546K** nominal.
13. Tech headcount +47% and revenue +79% (2017–24). Industrials & Defense is the one sector where staff grew faster than revenue (+30% vs +15%).
14. World market cap / GDP: **100% (2000) → 56% (2008) → 129% (2024)**.
15. Listed companies worldwide: 39,892 (2000) → 52,292 peak (2021) → 47,980 (2024).
16. US listed domestic companies: **8,090 (1996) → ~4,000–4,300 now**, roughly half.

## Story angles
- "Fewer, bigger, richer": profits double, market cap outruns GDP, and fewer US companies are listed.
- The decade's profit migration went to tech (structural) and energy (cyclical), away from telecoms and banks.
- India's giants were among the fastest growers despite a weaker rupee.
- Japan's lost decade, in dollars: 1.9% a year.

## Caveats
- **Survivorship bias:** the cohort is today's survivors. Failures, takeovers and dropouts (Credit Suisse, Evergrande, Russian firms) are missing, so growth is biased upward. Say "among today's largest companies...".
- Nominal US$ with no inflation adjustment (US CPI ~+32% over 2015–24); currency moves distort non-US firms.
- Approximate fiscal years (India and Japan end in March).
- Some single-year data oddities may remain; check company numbers against filings.
- Headcount is patchy before 2017.
- World Bank/WFE country listed-company series have coverage breaks (India, China, UK). Quote world and US figures with more confidence than other countries.
