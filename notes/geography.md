# Geography (geography.html): quotable facts

## World
- US: **26%** of world GDP, but **37%** of Global 2000 revenue, **42%** of profit, **55%** of market value (~$50T), **69%** of unicorn value (825 of 1,440 unicorns) and ~80% of measured listed market cap.
- China (mainland): **17%** of GDP, 275 firms, **15%** of revenue, but only **8%** of market value. Adding HK and Taiwan gives 358 firms and 18% of revenue.
- EU-27: 17.5% of GDP, 288 giants, 15% of revenue, but only 132 unicorns (**6%** of unicorn value).
- Japan: 180 giants (3rd), whose revenue equals **101% of Japan's GDP**, but only **6** unicorns.
- **61** countries have at least one Global 2000 firm. The top 5 countries hold 68% of revenue and the top 10 hold 82%.
- Market value ÷ revenue: US **2.6×**, India **1.8×**, EU 1.3×, Japan 1.0×, China **0.9×**.
- Over/under index (revenue share ÷ GDP share, Forbes view): Hong Kong **4.2×**, Luxembourg 2.3×, Switzerland 2.2×, Japan 1.9×, South Korea 1.7×, US 1.3×; China 0.80×, India **0.67×**. In the reconciled view Bermuda reaches 26× and Cayman 33×.
- GDP share vs revenue share rank correlation: ρ = 0.87 across 60 countries.
- **Zero Global 2000 firms** despite large GDP: Russia (~$2.2T, a list artefact of sanctions and delistings), Iran, Bangladesh, Pakistan, Iraq, Algeria, New Zealand, Nigeria, Ukraine.
- Regions: North America has 28% of GDP, 40% of revenue and 58% of market value. Asia-Pacific has 32% of GDP, 34% of revenue and only ~20% of value. The Middle East (& Central Asia, including North Africa) runs a **20.6%** net margin, about double the list's 9.2%. Africa has 17 firms and 0.3% of revenue against 2.3% of GDP.
- Stock markets relative to GDP: Switzerland 317%, Hong Kong 285%, US 273%, India **104%**, Germany 61%, China 58%.

## India
- **70** Global 2000 firms: **#4** by count, but **#9** by revenue ($1.32T, 35% of GDP), #7 by profit and #6 by market value ($2.35T).
- Indian giants average ~$19B of revenue, against a list average of $26B and a US average of ~$32B.
- Four of India's top five by revenue are state-controlled (LIC, Indian Oil, ONGC, SBI). Reliance is #1 ($114B revenue, $206B value).
- Value leaders: Reliance $206B, HDFC Bank $171B, TCS $146B (on only $30B revenue). LIC has $107B revenue but is valued at only $59B.
- Sector tilt: finance is 33% of Indian giant revenue (world 23%) and energy 30% (world 13%). Healthcare, industrials and food together are under 2%.
- India's 70 giants have a 9.6% net margin, against 9.2% for the list.
- **63 unicorns** (#4 after the US, China and UK) worth $144B. The largest are OYO ($9B), Dream11 ($8B), Razorpay ($7.5B) and Zepto ($7B). That is 17 unicorns per $1T of GDP, behind only the US and UK among big economies.
- Listed market cap is ~$3.9T across 567 measured firms (~104% of GDP). NSE ranks **#9** of 60 exchanges ($3.84T).
- Across all sources, 645 Indian firms carry $2.14T of measured revenue.

## Caveats
- Country means headquarters or domicile, not where revenue is earned or who owns the shares.
- Taiwan has no World Bank GDP, so it is excluded from the ÷GDP views.
- Vintages are mixed: Forbes May 2025, CompaniesMarketCap 2026, GDP 2024. Quote ratios as approximate.
- Listed-cap coverage is ~10k firms. It misses many small NSE/BSE and Chinese listings.
- Dollar conversion: a weaker rupee shrinks Indian firms in US$ terms.
- Some Indian-founded unicorns are domiciled in the US or Singapore and count there.
- Not on the map (too small or absent from Natural Earth 1:50m): Cayman Islands, Turks & Caicos, Gibraltar. They are still in the tables.

## Story angles
- "Many giants, small giants": India is 4th by count and 9th by revenue.
- "Investors pay a premium for India": 1.8× revenue vs China's 0.9×.
- "PSU heavyweights": revenue giants with modest valuations.
- "Missing sectors": no Indian manufacturing or pharma giants at the global scale.
- "Russia has vanished from the world's company list."
- "Headquarters economies": why Hong Kong, Switzerland and Luxembourg punch 2-4× their weight.
- "Europe builds big old firms, not new ones": 288 giants vs 132 unicorns.
