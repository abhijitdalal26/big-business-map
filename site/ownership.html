Ownership

One dollar in four of big-company revenue answers to a government

Stock markets show you most of the corporate world, not all of it. Of the 500 largest companies on earth, about a quarter of revenue sits with state-owned or state-controlled firms, and 128 giants have no listed shares at the group level. This page sorts the world's largest businesses by who owns them: shareholders, governments, families and foundations, or their own customers.

India: the PSU half of corporate India

India has 70 companies in the Forbes Global 2000. The 23 government-controlled ones (LIC, Indian Oil, ONGC, SBI, NTPC, the PSU banks) bring in about as much revenue as the 47 private ones combined, yet the market values them at a fraction.

Does the owner change the margin?

State-owned giants that are not listed earn thin margins. Many are utilities, grids, steelmakers, traders and postal services. Listed state companies (Aramco, the Chinese banks) look like their private peers. Family firms and traders look thin too, because trading companies book huge revenue on narrow spreads.

Unlisted state giants keep about 2.5 cents of profit per dollar of sales; listed peers keep about 10

America's largest private companies

The US has the deepest stock market on earth, yet Cargill, Koch, Mars and Publix still choose not to list. Forbes' list of the 200 largest US private companies adds up to about $2 trillion of revenue, roughly a tenth of what America's listed giants in the Global 2000 bring in.
