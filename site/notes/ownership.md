# Ownership: who owns the world's business? (notes for ownership.html)

Sources: Fortune Global 500 (2025, FY2024 revenue), Forbes Global 2000 (2025), Forbes America's Largest Private Companies (2025).
Ownership labels are curated by the atlas from public shareholding information (2025). Verify any single company before quoting.

## Quotable facts
1. **22.8%** of Fortune Global 500 revenue ($43.6T total) is in companies a government controls: 82 unlisted state groups ($6.8T) plus 28 listed but state-controlled firms ($3.2T). Including minority state stakes (20–50%) it is **26.5%**.
2. Listed companies without a major state stake: 325 of the 500, **67%** of revenue.
3. **128 of the 500 largest companies have no listed shares at group level** (state, private, mutual), with $9.8T of revenue between them.
4. Unlisted state groups bring in 15.5% of Fortune 500 revenue but only **4.6% of its profit**. Aggregate margin is **2.5%** (median 1.3%), and 10 of 82 lost money.
5. Listed state-controlled firms (Aramco, ICBC, CCB, SBI, LIC, Petrobras, Equinor...) have the **highest aggregate margin of any group, 13.0%**. State ownership on its own doesn't mean low profit; grids, posts, railways and steel do.
6. Listed firms with no big state stake: 10.1% aggregate margin, 7.0% median.
7. Study-pack test (state vs listed, mixed universe): median margin 2.0% vs 9.8%; difference in means −8.9 pp, 95% bootstrap CI [−10.3, −7.5].
8. **China:** 72 of its 111 Fortune 500 companies are state-controlled; about **75%** of their revenue is state-linked (incl. minority stakes). In the US it is almost zero (USPS, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac).
9. Largest unlisted company on earth: **State Grid, $555B**. In the Fortune list only Amazon and Walmart sell more. Then CNPC ($402B), Sinopec Group ($364B), Vitol (~$331B, curated), CSCEC parent ($290B), Trafigura ($240B).
10. **30** unlisted groups have revenue above $100B.
11. Across the Forbes Global 2000, **273 listed firms** have a government as controlling or major shareholder: **19%** of listed revenue (14% outright control) but only **~10% of market value**.
12. State share of each country's listed Global 2000 revenue (control + significant stake): Norway ~94%, Saudi Arabia ~90%, China ≥69% (floor), Hong Kong ~63%, India ~45%, US ~1%.
13. **India:** 23 of its 70 Global 2000 firms are PSUs. They bring in **45% of revenue** and 40% of profit but only **21% of market value**.
14. The PSU discount in one number: private Indian giants trade at **24.5× profit**, PSUs at **9.7×**. Margins are nearly equal (median 12.8% vs 12.9%).
15. Forbes' 200 largest US private companies: **~$2.0T revenue**, about a tenth of the US Global 2000 revenue. Cargill $154B, Koch $125B, Publix $60B, Mars $55B.
16. The Big Four accounting partnerships (Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG) bring in ~**$212B** combined and appear on no equity list.
17. Mutuals/co-ops are a third model: 20 Fortune 500 firms, $1.2T (State Farm, Nippon Life, Rabobank, Crédit Mutuel, BPCE, Edeka, REWE).
18. Foundations control Bosch (~94% of equity), Ingka/IKEA (100%), Novo Nordisk (~77% of votes), Carlsberg (~75% of votes) and ZF (~94%). India's equivalent is the Tata Trusts (~66% of Tata Sons).

## Story angles
- "The stock market is not the economy": about a quarter of the biggest businesses answer to governments rather than shareholders.
- India vs China: India listed its PSUs (small floats), China kept the parents unlisted. The outcome is similar: a heavily state-owned big-business sector.
- The PSU discount: same margins, less than half the valuation multiple.
- The disclosure gap: Vitol, Cargill, Schwarz and Aldi sell ~$800B a year and disclose very little.
- The Danish/German foundation model as protection against takeovers.

## Caveats
- Labels are curated and approximate. Stakes change (e.g. NatWest and AIB re-privatised in 2025).
- Fortune ranks parent groups and Forbes ranks listed entities, so never add them together (CNPC contains PetroChina).
- Fortune calls many Chinese SOE parents "Private" (meaning unlisted); they are relabelled state-owned here. The study pack's "56 state firms, $4.1T" counts only Fortune names outside Forbes and understates state ownership.
- China listed-SOE share: only firms with revenue ≥$10B were assessed (~93% of China's revenue), so it is a floor.
- Revenue is not value added. Traders and refiners inflate the private and state revenue shares.
- Curated revenue (Vitol, Schwarz, Aldi, ByteDance, Big Four, Ineos, CMA CGM) comes from press reports and disclosures for different fiscal years.
